# Future formats and prior art notes

Notes for planning new input formats, and the projects reviewed as references. The full record is in `git show V0.2.0:CLAUDE.md` under "Notes for future work".

## Future input formats

None is started; the scene (parts with a `brep` of a given format, levels from the source or ours, or both; external references) and the tessellator interface (`format: 'step' | 'iges' | 'brep'`) were designed for them.

| Format | Importer | Tessellation | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| JT (ISO 14306) | pure JS: binary segments, zlib or LZMA, compressed mesh coding, logical scene graph, properties; a large project | its meshes where the file holds them (a part's `lods`, `from: 'source'`), but they may be left out for the B-rep, mostly Parasolid's XT, which nothing we have can mesh exactly (see "JT" below) | not a priority (2026-11-07, see "JT" below); valuable mainly where Siemens' tools are used; OCCT has no reader; "shattered" assemblies need external references |
| IGES | pure JS: fixed 80-column ASCII, numbered types, no schema | OCCT: its IGES reader (`TKDEIGES`, not in our build) on subsets with renumbered pointers, or the MSBO form written as a STEP subset (see "IGES" below) | not a priority (2026-11-07, see "IGES" below); two B-rep forms: trimmed surfaces (type 144) with no shared topology, and the MSBO solid (type 186) with full topology; hierarchy by subfigures (308/408) and levels; truck has none |
| BREP (`.brep`) | one shape: OCCT reads it as it is; our own parser only for the unified model (see "OCCT BREP" below) | OCCT (`format: 'brep'`; `BRepTools` is in `TKBRep`, which our build links) | no names, colours, units or product structure; may carry triangulations; evaluated 2026-11-07 |
| OCCT documents (`.xbf`) | an OCCT WASM importer | OCCT, shapes as `brep` payloads | assembly, names, colours, layers |
| QIF (ISO 23952, `.qif`) | pure JS: XML (3MF's streaming tokenizer), the MBD sections: `FileUnits`, `GeometrySet`, `TopologySet`, `Product` (parts, assemblies, components, transforms), per-entity `color`/`transparency`/`hidden`; QIF files can also carry face meshes (`FaceMesh`, `SurfaceMeshSet`) | OCCT, the B-rep written as a STEP subset (see "QIF" below); its meshes, where present, as source levels | free schemas and permissive NIST samples; one-to-one with STEP's B-rep; no assembly samples found; evaluated 2026-11-06, not started |
| FreeCAD (`.FCStd`) | pure JS: unzip (3MF's zip reader), `Document.xml` (objects, tree, placements, links), `GuiDocument.xml` (visibility, colours) | OCCT, each `.brp` as a `brep` payload | the hard part is which objects are the final shapes; assemblies differ by version and workbench (see "FreeCAD" below); evaluated 2026-11-07 |

Suggested order: `.FCStd`, with `.brep` and `.xbf` nearly free once `format: 'brep'` exists; JT and IGES are not priorities.

P-curves in other formats: OCCT `.brep` edges carry p-curves in practice; JT's XT B-rep gives a fin an SP-curve only for a tolerant edge (JT v10.6, Annex F); IGES type 142 carries parameter-space and model-space curves with a preference flag (5.3, 4.32), type 508 edge-uses' parameter curves optional (4.145).

## Across formats

Curves, surfaces and B-rep compared (STEP, QIF, IGES, JT's three forms; 2026-11-07), against two STEP baselines, which differ:

- **AP242's advanced face** (`advanced_face` in the AP242 MIM, `stepcode/data/ap242/242_mim_lf.exp`), the B-rep CAD systems exchange: face surfaces only `elementary_surface`, `swept_surface` (linear extrusion, revolution, surface-curve swept, fixed-reference swept) or `b_spline_surface` (`advanced_face_geometry_select`: no `offset_surface`); edge geometry only `line`, `conic`, `polyline`, `surface_curve` (with its subtypes `intersection_curve` and `seam_curve`, whose 3D curve is still explicit) or `b_spline_curve` (WR2: no `trimmed_curve`, `composite_curve` or `offset_curve_3d`); vertices `vertex_point`s. OCCT reads more than this (offset surfaces and curves, trimmed curves). Part 42 is a resource: AP242's MIM includes its geometry and topology and adds representation subtypes whose WHERE rules say what each may hold: `advanced_brep_shape_representation` (manifold solids and solids with voids, all faces advanced), `manifold_surface_shape_representation` (shell-based surface models, faces advanced or `face_surface` passing `msf_surface_check`, which also admits offset surfaces and replicas), `faceted_brep_shape_representation`, `geometrically_bounded_surface_shape_representation` and `..._wireframe_...` (geometric sets with no topology: trimmed and composite curves, `curve_bounded_surface`, `rectangular_trimmed_surface`), wireframe models, CSG, tessellated; a plain `shape_representation` has no such rules.

**The corpus** (517 STEP files, 2026-11-07; `corpus_checks/step_geometry.ts`, its log beside it): B-rep faces are 81114 advanced faces (456 files) and 2081 `face_surface`s (15 files); their surfaces are only planes, cylinders, cones, spheres, tori (10 degenerate), B-splines (rational or not, some quasi-uniform), linear extrusions and revolutions; their edges' curves only lines, circles, ellipses, 8 hyperbolas, 4 parabolas, B-splines, surface curves (12211) and seam curves (338), plus 2 trimmed curves in one file (outside the advanced face's rules; 43 more edges and faces reference missing instances). No offset surface or curve, intersection curve, implicit curve, locally refined spline or other Part 42 extra appears anywhere. Solids sit in `advanced_brep_shape_representation` (334 files) or a plain `shape_representation` (137 files, advanced faces all the same), surface models in `manifold_surface_shape_representation` (108 files). Geometry no face uses is PMI (polylines, trimmed curves, planes in `draughting_model`s), wireframe (`geometrically_bounded_wireframe_shape_representation`, 65 files: trimmed curves, lines, circles), surface models without topology (`geometrically_bounded_surface_shape_representation`, 50 files: curve-bounded surfaces with boundary curves of trimmed curves, B-splines, lines), construction geometry (`constructive_geometry_representation`, 6 files: datum planes, axes), CSG (one plant file: blocks, revolved and extruded area solids) and construction history (two files' Boolean results). P-curves' 2D geometry (in `definitional_representation`s) is lines, B-splines and trimmed curves.
- **Part 42's geometry as AP242 includes it**, much broader: implicit curves (`implicit_intersection_curve`, `implicit_projected_curve`, `implicit_silhouette_curve`), locally refined splines, Dupin cyclides, swept-disk and surface-curve-swept surfaces, CSG, and procedural blends on solids (`solid_with_constant_radius_edge_blend`, `solid_with_variable_radius_edge_blend`; features, not B-rep faces).

Outside the advanced face's vocabulary, from the other formats:

- **Exact conversions** (no loss): polynomial-basis splines (QIF `Spline13`/`Spline23`, IGES 112/114) and composite curves (QIF `Aggregate13`, IGES 102) to B-splines; implicit conic coefficients (IGES 104) to an ellipse, hyperbola or parabola; ruled surfaces (QIF `Ruled23`, IGES 118) to B-splines; transforms on any entity, mirrors included (IGES 124), baked into the geometry; QIF's cone by diameters and length to a semi-angle.
- **Approximations only:** implicit intersection curves and rolling-ball and cliff-edge blends (XT; STEP's implicit curves aren't advanced-face geometry and are another definition); blend-boundary surfaces (XT, construction only); variable offset curves (IGES 130 forms 2-3); ULP's quantized geometry (lossy by design).
- **Topology:** faces without shared topology (IGES 144/143, to be sewn); poles inside a loop (IGES; dropped); faces with no outer loop identified (IGES, QIF; to be worked out) or bounded only by the surface's extent (QIF `hasOuter` false; STEP faces need at least one bound); JT B-rep trim loops in parameter space, model-space curves possibly absent (computed); ULP's derived curves (G.1.4).
- **Tolerances:** per vertex and edge (XT tolerant edges, QIF) where STEP has one uncertainty per context; OCCT's own model holds a tolerance per vertex, edge and face.
- **P-curves:** optional in IGES and QIF, SP-curves only on tolerant edges in XT, primary in JT B-rep and ULP; reusable only where the surface's parameterization matches STEP's (B-splines, and analytic surfaces to be checked case by case).
- **Not geometry we read:** XT's foreign (PE) geometry and mesh surfaces.

## OCCT BREP

OCCT's own shape format, written by `BRepTools` (ASCII, header `CASCADE Topology V1`-`V3`: V2 adds the UV end points of curves on surfaces, V3 triangulation normals) and `BinTools` (binary, `Open CASCADE Topology V1`-`V4`). The ASCII form is specified in `third_party/OCCT/dox/specification/brep_format.md` (BNF-like, with OCCT's source; LGPL, part of the pinned checkout, so nothing to fetch); the binary form only by the source. Samples: 2811 in `test_corpus/third_party/gkv311-opencascade-dataset/brep/` and 37 in `third_party/OCCT/data/`, many cut from STEP files (`1.stp_face1015.brep`); and the `.brp` files in every FreeCAD document. Over the first two (2026-11-07, a section-level scan): 2840 ASCII (2800 V1, 40 V3), 8 binary; 64175 faces, 3315 solids, 1493 compounds, 5 compsolids; triangulations in 914 files; p-curves in 2280 of the 2796 with 3D curves (OCCT computes them on planes). Their geometry (a token-level parse of the curve and surface sections by the BNF, which consumed every section of all 2913 ASCII files, FreeCAD's `.brp`s included, exactly): surfaces mostly planes (45565), B-splines (12931) and cylinders (9774), then rectangular trimmed, Bezier, extrusion, revolution, torus, cone, sphere; **offset surfaces 52 in 26 files and offset 3D curves 44 in 7**, nearly all OCCT's own regression cases (`bugNNNNN`, `OCCnnn`), their bases B-splines (33), Bezier (6), tori (6), trimmed surfaces and revolutions, the curves' bases trimmed curves and ellipses; none in FreeCAD's samples. Offsets elsewhere: none in the STEP corpus (`corpus_checks/step_geometry.log`; outside AP242's advanced face anyway), two IGES offset surfaces (type 140) in the OCCT dataset, none in the Onshape IGES exports. They arise from offset and thickness operations in kernels that keep them (OCCT's `BRepOffset`, so FreeCAD's Part Offset and Thickness; Parasolid, ACIS). Without OCCT: an offset of a plane, cylinder, cone, sphere or torus is the same kind of surface (radius or position changed: exact), of a line or circle a line or circle; an offset of a B-spline or Bezier is no B-spline, and though each point evaluates simply (the basis point plus the distance along its normal), the surface can be irregular: it folds (cusps, normals flipping) where the distance exceeds the radius of curvature on the concave side, regular only where (1 - d k1)(1 - d k2) > 0 with the principal curvatures signed by the offset's side; distant parts of the basis closer than twice the distance give sheets that cross; and it is undefined where the basis's normal is (poles, collapsed edges). A valid face uses only a trimmed region its modeller left regular, but that is not guaranteed. So a unified model would keep such offsets as a kind of their own: passed to OCCT where it is the backend; for other consumers checked for regularity over the face's trimmed domain (sampled curvatures, the sign above; global crossings are harder), with a diagnostic where it fails; approximation by B-splines (error-bounded, dense knots where the curvature radius nears the distance, impossible across a fold) an open problem, not textbook work.

**The ontology:**

- **Shapes:** a graph of shared shapes: vertex, edge, wire, face, shell, solid, compsolid, compound. Each use of a sub-shape has an orientation (forward, reversed, internal, external: the last two for non-manifold use, such as an edge inside a face) and a location (a transform, itself a product of shared elementary transforms with powers): instancing at every level, not only for assembly components. A compound is any collection, so a whole assembly can be one shape, but nothing names its members.
- **Tolerances** on every vertex, edge and face. Flags per edge (same parameter, same range, degenerate) and per shape (closed, orientable, convex, ...).
- **Edges:** a 3D curve with its parameter range; per face, a p-curve with its range (a seam edge two p-curves on the same face, with their continuity); the continuity across the edge between two faces (`C0`-`CN`, `G1`, `G2`); a 3D polygon or polygons on the faces' triangulations. **Vertices:** a point, plus its parameter on curves and surfaces. **Faces:** a surface with a location, a natural-restriction flag, a tolerance, and triangulations (with UV parameters, normals in V3).
- **Geometry:** 3D curves line, circle, ellipse, parabola, hyperbola, Bezier, B-spline, trimmed, offset; surfaces plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus, linear extrusion, revolution, Bezier, B-spline, rectangular trimmed, offset; 2D curves the same kinds as the 3D ones.
- **Not in it:** names, colours, units (FreeCAD uses mm), product structure, implicit curves and blends, polynomial-basis splines, ruled surfaces.

**Against STEP in practice:** the geometry kinds are the advanced face's plus Bezier, trimmed and offset (OCCT's STEP reader maps the corpus's STEP geometry onto them one to one). What OCCT holds that STEP files don't: tolerances per entity (STEP has one uncertainty per context), p-curves on every non-planar face (OCCT computes those a STEP file lacks), edge regularity, internal and external orientations, locations on any sub-shape (STEP places only representations, through mapped items and `REPRESENTATION_RELATIONSHIP`s), triangulations beside the exact geometry. What STEP holds that BREP doesn't: everything above the shape (products, names, colours, layers, units, PMI, validation properties).

**Import and export of a single solid:**

- **Import:** a part whose `brep` payload is the file itself with `format: 'brep'`, read in the OCCT wrapper by `BRepTools` (in `TKBRep`, already linked), meshed as a STEP subset is; face ids by the order OCCT's explorer first meets each face (no STEP ids). Diagnostics for what isn't there (units assumed mm). Its triangulations, where present, could be a source level of detail. Truck can't read it. A parser of our own is needed only for the unified model, or for a backend other than OCCT.
- **Export:** writing BREP needs, for each face, p-curves and tolerances that STEP files often lack, so either OCCT writes it (the STEP subset read, then `BRepTools` writing, in the wrapper: cheap) or our writer works from the unified model once that has p-curves and tolerances (computing p-curves is projection geometry of our own).

**For the unified B-rep model:** OCCT's ontology is close to the model that model needs: the practical STEP vocabulary as a subset, the tolerances, p-curves, edge regularity and orientations that B-rep operations need, sharing with locations, and exactly what the OCCT backend consumes, so that backend's interface could map one to one. What it lacks would be our extension kinds: XT's implicit curves and blends, polynomial splines, ruled surfaces (all converted before OCCT sees them), and the scene's own names, colours and product structure, kept above the shapes as now. Its concepts can be taken from the specification; its classes and code can't (`licensing.md`).

## FreeCAD

**Documents and samples:** no specification beyond the wiki (`File_Format_FCStd` is thin; `Std_Part`, `Std_LinkMake`, `Assembly_Workbench` describe the containers and links) and FreeCAD's source (LGPL-2.1-or-later; read for behaviour, never translated). In `third_party/freecad/`, fetched by `third_party/pull.sh`: those wiki pages at pinned revisions (wikitext, CC BY 3.0) and four documents from FreeCAD's repository at a pinned commit: `AssemblyExample` (the 1.x Assembly workbench), `EngineBlock` (Part workbench booleans), `PartDesignExample` (a Body of features), all saved by a 1.x development build (`26.3R48259`), and `ModelFromV021` (0.21). Also `test_corpus/third_party/occt-import-js/test/testfiles/cube-fcstd/` and `cube-units/` (small cubes).

**The file:** a zip of `Document.xml` (every object's type, name and properties), `GuiDocument.xml` (each object's view provider: visibility, appearance), a BREP file per stored shape (`Shape001.Shape.brp`, ASCII V1 in the samples; 1.x also keeps a topological-naming element map, `*.Map.txt`), binary property files (appearance lists), a thumbnail.

- **Geometry:** every shape-holding object stores its `Shape` (`<Part file="X.Shape.brp"/>`), intermediate results included: in `EngineBlock` 36 objects, two of them visible results; in `PartDesignExample` a Body (visible) over 12 features and 7 sketches (hidden). Which objects are the final shapes is the hard part: a `PartDesign::Body`'s shape is its tip's, so its features are skipped; a Part feature consumed by another (a boolean's operands) is an intermediate; sketches, Draft 2D objects, origins and datums aren't solids. Visibility (a view state in `GuiDocument.xml`) is the best hint, not a rule.
- **Tree:** containers with a placement (`App::Part`; `PartDesign::Body`; the Assembly workbench's `Assembly::AssemblyObject`, an `App::Part` subtype holding links and a joint group) and without (`App::DocumentObjectGroup`, a folder); each listing its children (`Group`, a link list). Instances are `App::Link`s: `LinkedObject` (in this document, or another file: an external reference), `LinkPlacement`, `ScaleVector` (a non-uniform scale is possible), `ElementCount` for arrays. In `AssemblyExample`, 13 base shapes in a hidden folder and an assembly of 13 links placing them. A placement is a position and a quaternion (`Px`-`Pz`, `Q0`-`Q3` as x, y, z, w). Labels are the names to show; object names are ids. Third-party assembly workbenches (A2plus, Assembly3, Assembly4) build their trees in their own ways, not covered.
- **Appearance:** 1.x `ShapeAppearance` (a material list file per object: a count, then per material packed RGBA ambient, diffuse, specular and emissive colours, shininess, transparency, and version-dependent fields; one material per face when faces differ), 0.21 `ShapeColor` plus a `DiffuseColor` colour list (per face) and `Transparency`. Per-face lists follow the shape's face order (OCCT's explorer order of distinct faces). Links can override their target's materials (`OverrideMaterial`, `MaterialList`).

**Implications:**

- **Import** is pure TypeScript apart from meshing: our zip and XML readers (`3mf-import`), the containers and links as components and occurrences (placements composed, labels as names), each final shape a part with a `format: 'brep'` payload (the BREP support above), face colours from the appearance lists by face order (face ids numbered the same way), units mm. The rules for final shapes are the risk, to be built from samples of each workbench and checked against FreeCAD's own export of the same documents (STEP from FreeCAD as the oracle, where it's available).
- **Export** of an assembly tree: a `Document.xml` of `Part::Feature`s (one per distinct part, hidden, in a folder as `AssemblyExample` does) and an `App::Part` tree of `App::Link`s placing them, a `GuiDocument.xml` with visibility and `ShapeAppearance`, and a BREP file per part (written by OCCT from the STEP subset, or by our own writer from the unified model). FreeCAD opens such a document as plain shapes, without parametric history; enough for an assembly to be edited and re-placed there.

## IGES

**Not a priority** (the user's decision, 2026-11-07). Modern systems export STEP, so IGES matters mainly for legacy systems and data, and that data is mostly the trimmed-surface form, which needs sewing that neither backend does and that is hard to do well (below).

The last version is IGES 5.3 (1996, ANSI 1996-2006; nothing since): `third_party/iges/`, fetched by `third_party/pull.sh` (US PRO's free download, from the Internet Archive since US PRO closed in 2006; "All Rights Reserved", so fetched, not committed), with a text version. No schemas: the entities are defined in the document (each with its directory-entry and parameter-data fields). Section numbers below are 5.3's. Sample files: the user's Onshape exports in `test_corpus/models/*/*.iges` and the 87 in `test_corpus/third_party/gkv311-opencascade-dataset/iges/` (with two in `third_party/OCCT/data/iges/`); IGES has no CAx-IF-style test suite.

**Two B-rep forms** (checked 2026-11-07, entity counts from the directory entries):

- **Trimmed surfaces** (type 144, or 143 with type 141 boundaries): a surface and its outer and inner boundaries, each a curve on the surface (type 142) holding a parameter-space curve, a model-space curve or both, with a flag for which the sender prefers. No vertices, edges or shells: each face's boundary is its own, and adjacent faces aren't connected, so a solid has to be sewn. Nearly all the OCCT dataset's files (CATIA V4 and V5, NX, Pro/ENGINEER, SolidWorks 2010, Rhino, ANSA, Euclid; IGES 5.1-5.3).
- **Manifold solid B-rep object** (MSBO, type 186, 4.49; with vertex lists 502, edge lists 504, loops 508, faces 510 and shells 514, 4.143-4.147; all marked "untested" in 5.3): full topology, close to STEP's. Every Onshape export (written by HOOPS Exchange as IGES 5.1: 432 solids, 23692 faces, no type 144), and a few in the dataset (Inventor, I-DEAS, Spatial, C3D, OCCT).

OCCT's IGES reader doesn't sew: its shape processing (`resources/XSTEPResource/IGES`) is `FixShape` only, and nothing in `TKDEIGES` calls `BRepBuilderAPI_Sewing`, so type 144 faces come in unconnected. Viewing would do without sewing (each face meshes alone, cracks along shared boundaries possible), but the other uses wouldn't: a manifold mesh for 3MF, B-rep operations on the loaded solid, or writing it out as a STEP solid all need high-quality sewing. OCCT's sewing (`BRepBuilderAPI_Sewing`, in `TKTopAlgo`, which our build already links) is unproven for us: its own suite, `tests/sewing/`, has 700 cases (nearly all on its `.rle` shape files, none read from IGES; 17 `TODO` markers of known failures), and OCCT's mesher already fails on a share of real faces (`backends.md`). Measuring it on the dataset's type 144 files (free edges left, shells closed, solids made) would be the first step if IGES were taken up.

**The MSBO form against STEP:**

- **Topology maps one to one.** Vertex (an index into a vertex list) -> `VERTEX_POINT`; edge (an index into an edge list: curve, start and end vertices; natural orientation along its curve) -> `EDGE_CURVE` with `same_sense` true; edge-use (edge, orientation, optional p-curves, 4.145) -> `ORIENTED_EDGE`; loop -> `EDGE_LOOP`; face (surface, loops, an outer-loop flag that may say none is identified) -> `ADVANCED_FACE` with `FACE_OUTER_BOUND`/`FACE_BOUND`; shell (form 1 closed, form 2 open, each face-use with an orientation flag) -> `CLOSED_SHELL`/`OPEN_SHELL`, the flag as the face's `same_sense`; MSBO (a shell and void shells, each with an orientation flag) -> `MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP` or `BREP_WITH_VOIDS`. The vertex and edge lists are syntax only.
- **Doesn't map:** a pole, an edge-use naming a vertex instead of an edge (STEP has `VERTEX_LOOP` only as a whole loop; drop it, OCCT adds degenerate edges itself); a face with no outer loop identified (pick or compute the outer one).
- **Curves allowed for edges (4.144):** line (110) -> `LINE` (a segment: trimmed by the edge's vertices); circular arc (100, in a plane at a height, placed by its transform) -> `CIRCLE`; conic arc (104, as implicit coefficients A-F) -> `ELLIPSE`, `HYPERBOLA` or `PARABOLA`, axes computed; composite (102) -> an exact B-spline (joined; `COMPOSITE_CURVE` isn't allowed as an advanced face's edge geometry, see "Across formats"); paths and closed planar curves (106 forms 11, 12, 63) -> `POLYLINE`; rational B-spline (126) -> `B_SPLINE_CURVE_WITH_KNOTS` (knots listed with repeats, folded into distinct knots and multiplicities; rational as a complex instance); parametric spline (112, piecewise polynomial) -> a B-spline, exactly; offset curve (130) -> `OFFSET_CURVE_3D` only for a constant offset (its linear and function-of-arc-length offsets have no STEP form), and that not as edge geometry of an advanced face, so a B-spline approximation either way.
- **Surfaces allowed for faces (4.146):** plane, cylindrical, conical, spherical and toroidal surfaces (190-198; Onshape writes form 1, parameterized with a reference direction) -> `PLANE`, `CYLINDRICAL_SURFACE`, `CONICAL_SURFACE`, `SPHERICAL_SURFACE`, `TOROIDAL_SURFACE`; surface of revolution (120, between two angles) -> `SURFACE_OF_REVOLUTION` (the full turn; the face's loops trim it); tabulated cylinder (122, its directrix swept to a point) -> `SURFACE_OF_LINEAR_EXTRUSION`; rational B-spline (128) -> `B_SPLINE_SURFACE_WITH_KNOTS`; offset surface (140) -> `OFFSET_SURFACE` (outside AP242's advanced face, but OCCT reads it); parametric spline surface (114) -> a B-spline, exactly; ruled surface form 1 (118) -> a B-spline (no AP242 face surface; as for QIF).
- **Transforms:** any entity may point to a transformation matrix (124), chained; form 1 is a reflection. Bake them into the geometry (placements moved, B-splines' points transformed; reflections flip the analytic surfaces' axes, which STEP placements can't hold otherwise).
- **P-curves:** optional per edge-use; usable where the surface's parameterization matches STEP's (B-splines; form 1 analytic surfaces to check), else dropped for OCCT to compute, as for QIF.
- **Also not B-rep:** the CSG solids (150-168 primitives, 180 Boolean tree, 184 solid assembly), which map onto AP242's CSG (`csg_solid`, `boolean_result`, `block`, ...), not onto its B-rep.

**Product structure and appearance:** a subfigure definition (308) and its instances (408, with a translation and a uniform scale, plus any transform matrix) -> components and occurrences (Onshape: 363 definitions, 1395 instances); colour by a directory-entry colour number (1-8 predefined) or a colour definition (314); names from the name property (406 form 15) or the directory entry's label; levels (layers) in the directory entry; units and the model's scale from the global section.

## QIF

Evaluated 2026-11-06 for part geometry, appearance and assembly structure (no PMI, plans or results); nothing built.

- **Documents:** the ANSI QIF 3.0 standard (2018; ISO 23952:2020 is the same version, paywalled at ISO) is free from qifstandards.org, behind a sign-up form on the site but also as a direct download (the PDF and its XSDs, in `third_party/qif/standard/` with a text version; `qif-community` in `third_party/qif/qif-community/`, both fetched by `third_party/pull.sh`); the schemas alone (22 XSD files: `Geometry`, `Topology`, `QIFProduct`, `Visualization`, `Units`, `PrimitivesPD`, ...) are in `github.com/QualityInformationFramework/qif-community` (`bindings/CPP-Kramer/schema/`, Boost Software License for the repository; the XSDs' own header claims no rights), browsable at `qualityinformationframework.github.io/qif3-browser`. The XSDs document every type, which is enough for geometry, topology and product structure; the standard's text would still help for parameterizations the XSDs only name.
- **Samples:** six NIST PMI test models in QIF (`samples/NISTmodels/`, CTC 1, 3, 5, FTC 6, 8, 9, written by Capvidia's MBDVidia; Boost licence), three more in `bindings/CPP-Kramer/data/` and small test blocks; all single parts. The corpus has the same NIST models as STEP, a ready cross-check: `nist_ctc_01` has 117 faces, 318 edges, 140 loops, 90 circular arcs and 57 cylinders in both. No assembly sample was found; assemblies would need a commercial exporter (MBDVidia, CAD systems' QIF export).
- **No reader to lean on:** OCCT has none; nothing permissively licensed tessellates QIF B-rep.
- **B-rep, entity for entity like STEP's:** `Vertex` (point) -> `VERTEX_POINT`; `Edge` (3D curve, `VertexBeg`/`VertexEnd` in the curve's parameter order) -> `EDGE_CURVE` with same sense true; `CoEdge` (`EdgeOriented` with `turned`, optional `Curve12` p-curve) -> `ORIENTED_EDGE`; `Loop` -> `EDGE_LOOP`; `Face` (surface, loops, `hasOuter`, `turned`) -> `ADVANCED_FACE` (same sense = not turned, the outer loop first as `FACE_OUTER_BOUND`); `Shell`, `Body` -> shells and `MANIFOLD_SOLID_BREP` (or a shell-based surface model). So each QIF body can be written as a STEP subset and meshed by the OCCT backend unchanged, with QIF ids as the `#` ids (face ids for picking, measures, failures).
- **Curves** (`Curve13`): `Segment13` -> `LINE`; `ArcCircular13` (centre, `DirBeg`, normal, radius) -> `CIRCLE`; `ArcConic13` -> `ELLIPSE`/`PARABOLA`/`HYPERBOLA`; `Nurbs13` (order, full knot vector, optional weights) -> `B_SPLINE_CURVE_WITH_KNOTS` (knots folded into distinct values and multiplicities; rational as the complex entity); `Polyline13` -> `POLYLINE`; `Spline13` (piecewise polynomials: breakpoints, per-segment orders and coefficients) and `Aggregate13` (a chain of curves) -> an exact B-spline (basis conversion, joining; `COMPOSITE_CURVE` isn't allowed as an advanced face's edge geometry).
- **Surfaces** (`Surface`): `Plane23` (origin, `DirU`, `DirV`), `Cylinder23`, `Cone23` (diameters at bottom and top, length, axis, sweep), `Sphere23`, `Torus23` -> the STEP elementary surfaces (the cone's semi-angle from its diameters and length; signs to be checked on real files); `Nurbs23` -> `B_SPLINE_SURFACE_WITH_KNOTS`; `Extrude23` -> `SURFACE_OF_LINEAR_EXTRUSION`; `Revolution23` -> `SURFACE_OF_REVOLUTION`; `Offset23` -> `OFFSET_SURFACE` (outside AP242's advanced face, but OCCT reads it); `Spline23` -> an exact B-spline; `Ruled23` (two curves) -> a B-spline surface of degree 1 across (curves made compatible: degree elevation, knot merging; AP242 has no ruled face surface, only `RULED_SURFACE_SWEPT_AREA_SOLID`). The B-spline algorithms are textbook (Piegl and Tiller), not OCCT's.
- **Parameterizations differ:** QIF curves carry an explicit `domain`, surfaces their own (`scaleU`, `scaleV`, `turnedV`, a cone's swept angle), so QIF p-curves aren't STEP's. Drop them and let OCCT project (as it does for STEP files without p-curves), or keep them only where the parameterizations match (planes, some elementary surfaces).
- **Risks:** faces without an explicit outer loop (`hasOuter` false: the surface's natural boundary), which STEP can't write as such: boundary edges built from the surface's domain (seams, poles); `Spline` and `Ruled` conversions; tolerant bodies (per-vertex and per-edge `tolerance`) where STEP has one uncertainty; the arc's sweep direction against its normal and the cone's conventions, to verify on real files; no assemblies to test.
- **Product structure and appearance:** `Part` (bodies), `Assembly` (component ids), `Component` (an instance of a part or assembly, a `Transform` reference, a UUID), a root part or assembly, `AsmPath` for instance paths; maps onto components, occurrences and placed parts. Colours are RGB attributes on drawables (bodies, faces, edges, ...), with `transparency` and `hidden`: per-face materials as STEP's styles become. Units from `FileUnits`.

## JT

**Not a priority** (the user's decision, 2026-11-07). JT is used mostly with Siemens' JT toolkit, which includes Parasolid, and its usual precise geometry is Parasolid's XT B-rep, whose implicit blends and intersection curves we can't reproduce exactly without Parasolid. Outside the settings where Siemens' CAD and PLM tools are used, JT has little value. The alternative, an importer of JT's meshes alone, avoids Parasolid but still needs JT's container and codecs, and can't count on the meshes being in the file.

**Meshes are not always there.** v10.6 section 4.2 lists the LSG and Shape LOD segments as "mandatory" and every B-rep form as optional (v10.0's list says neither; v9.5 has no such list). But the NULL Shape Node (5.3.17) is a shape with no geometry, "often used as 'proxy/placeholder' nodes within the serialized LSG when the actual Shape LOD data is run time generated (therefore not persisted)": a file can hold the scene graph, placeholders and a B-rep per part, for the reader to tessellate on load (as Siemens' toolkit does with Parasolid). The best practices (v10.0 section 14.1, v9.5 section 9.1) load only the table of contents and the LSG up front, with B-rep and LOD segments late-loaded when needed. The user remembers JT files of about 2016 often holding only B-rep; how common that is now is unchecked (no sample files).

**Three B-rep forms** (v10.6), and how each would map onto what we hand OCCT as STEP (as QIF's would, above):

- **XT B-rep** (segment type 17, and MultiXT, type 30, for several parts; Annex F): Parasolid's transmit format embedded, the current and recommended form. Maps: the topology (body, region, shell, face, loop, fin as `ORIENTED_EDGE`, edge, vertex); lines, circles, ellipses, B-curves, polylines, trimmed curves; planes, cylinders, cones, spheres, tori, B-surfaces, offset, swept (extrusion) and spun (revolution) surfaces; SP-curves as p-curves; colour, transparency and names (system attributes, F.3.7). Doesn't map: intersection curves (implicit: two surfaces plus chart points, evaluated by Parasolid's own method), blended-edge surfaces (rolling-ball and cliff-edge blends, implicit: a spine that is itself the intersection of two offset surfaces, and a radius), blend-boundary construction surfaces, foreign (PE) geometry, mesh surfaces, tolerant edges and vertices with tolerances of their own (STEP has one per model). Implicit curves and blends could only be approximated (fitted B-splines within a tolerance). The transmit format itself, schema-driven, is a large reader of its own; OCCT has none.
- **JT B-rep** (type 2; Annex K, deprecated since JT IAP V2, for legacy content): JT's own compressed B-rep. Maps well: regions, shells with an anti-hole flag, faces with a reverse-normal flag, loops, co-edges with a reversed flag, edges, vertices; NURBS-only geometry ("currently only NURBS ... is supported", for surfaces and model-space curves alike) as STEP B-splines, rational or not. Trim loops are defined in parameter space, so where a file lacks model-space edge curves they'd be computed or written as surface curves. Older files only.
- **ULP** (Annex G): "semi-precise", at most 0.01% of the part's size or 0.1 mm off, whichever is smaller, about a tenth the size of a JT file with B-rep and LODs. Plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus and NURBS surfaces, line, circle and NURBS curves in both spaces, in XT's conventions: all map, with no implicit geometry. But much of it isn't stored: G.1.4 "Information Recovery" derives p-curves from the surface's domain and some model-space curves by projecting p-curves onto their surface, geometry code of our own before OCCT sees it, behind heavy quantization and entropy coding.

The STEP B-rep segment (type 33) is undefined; LWPA holds only a part's analytic surfaces.

## JT specifications

In `third_party/jt/`, fetched by `third_party/pull.sh`, with text versions.

- **v10.6** (Rev D, 2026-02-10, 493 pages, plus the PMI property strings list) is the primary reference: file structure, the logical scene graph, levels of detail including TopoMesh, every compression scheme with reference decoder code (Annexes B-D), the XT B-rep (Annex F), ULP, Smart Topology Table and LWPA segments, wireframe, procedural geometry (Annex M), deprecated B-rep and PMI segments (Annexes K, L).
- **v9.5** (Rev A, 2010) is required too: v9 and v10 share the scene graph, TopoMesh coder and arithmetic/bit-length codecs, but v10 replaced the compression layer (new Int32 and Int64 CDPs, LZMA against zlib), and ISO 14306:2012 is v9.5, so v9 files are common. **v10.0** (Rev B, 2015) is a cross-check.
- **Gaps:** the STEP B-rep segment (type 33) is undefined; no v8 specification exists; real files from current writers are needed.
- **License terms:** anyone may write and distribute software that reads and interprets JT data, with a copyright notice; the documents may not be redistributed (hence fetched). The permission excludes "incorporating, integrating, or combining" JT into another format: converting JT to glTF most plausibly counts as interpreting, but review it before shipping a converter rather than a viewer. Siemens' patent clarification notice grants a royalty-free license to US 8019788 and US 2011/0199382 for compliant JT 9.5+ software.

## Reference projects and prior art

Reviewed 2026-10-16 (commits in `third_party/README.md`); none is a dependency except OCCT, stepcode and truck.

- **truck** (Rust, Apache-2.0): a full independent pipeline (ruststep, B-rep, NURBS, `spade` CDT meshing, assemblies). **ruststep**: Part 21 parser and `espr`; experimental.
- **web-ifc** (C++ -> WASM, MPL-2.0): fast browser Part 21 parser for IFC and trimmed NURBS; the closest architectural analogue.
- **verb** (MIT): NURBS, no trimmed surfaces. **tinyspline** (MIT), **tinynurbs** (BSD-3): NURBS evaluation. **BRL-CAD `step-g`** (LGPL), **SISL/GoTools** (AGPL), **SolveSpace** (GPL): reference only. **cdt2d**, **poly2tri.js**, **earcut**: 2D triangulation.
- **No open-source equivalent of OCCT's `ShapeFix`** exists: healing is where a pure-JS tessellator would need original work or accept lower robustness.
- **SFA** (NIST, Tcl) walks product structure through closed IFCsvr and tessellates through STP2X3D (OCCT).
- Other OCCT WASM packages: `occt-wasm`, `@bitbybit-dev/occt`, `opencascade.js` (last published 2023).
- Not obtainable: the CAx-IF round-robin files (members only) and the ISO standards; the corpus stands in for the first, the EXPRESS schemas and OCCT's behaviour for the second.
