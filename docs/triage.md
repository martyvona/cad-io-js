# Mesh triage

How a tessellation's meshes are judged and chosen when no backend meshes every face to all the requirements: the criteria every face's mesh is measured by, the targets (triage profiles) that say what each consumer tolerates, and how a mesh is picked between a primary and a fallback backend. This is a design, decided with the user (2026-11-24), built: the record per face (below), the targets, and the choice by them; what exists today is in `docs/backends.md` (the fallback, the judge, the area oracle) and `docs/own-tessellator.md` (tess-cadio's own checks and keepDefects).

## Principles

- **Generic:** triage knows nothing of any backend or pair of backends. It compares records of meshes, whoever made them.
- **Triage only selects:** it picks among the meshes the backends deliver and never asks for another meshing. What a backend knows about its own failures (tess-cadio's lantern retry, its point-cap freeze, its repairs of turned triangles) it does inside itself, before it reports.
- **Backends are told the true limits:** the meshing profile's deflections, and where a target sets them the smallest angle and the longest edge, so a backend's own retries aim at what the consumer needs. A backend that can't aim at a limit (OCCT and SMLib have no longest edge) is only judged by it.
- **A backend reports, it doesn't refuse:** tess-cadio delivers its best manifold mesh of every face it can mesh manifold, with its record; triage decides whether it's used. A face no backend meshes manifold is a hole.
- **A fallback mesh that meets every true limit is used.** Triage's rules below only matter where neither backend's mesh does.

## The criteria

Each face's mesh is measured by these, in this order (the order is also the tie-break's, below). The true limit is what a mesh should meet; the target sets, per criterion, the multiple of it past which a mesh is warned of and the multiple past which it is an error.

| | Criterion | True limit | Measured for foreign backends |
|---|---|---|---|
| k | Complete: the face has triangles (a warning where only the sliver fill meshed it, judged on the filled mesh) | | always |
| a | Manifold: (a.1) within the face, its boundary edges aside; (a.2) sewn into its shell, every edge's polyline shared exactly with its other uses after the exact join (a seam's within the face) | | always |
| j | Oriented: a closed shell's mesh faces out | | always |
| b | Area against the area oracle's exact area, where it trusts one (its error, the boundary's bound included, within 1 % of it: `JUDGE_RELATIVE`, a third of the 3 % short limit) | the judge's: short by more than max(3 % of A, d x P), over by more than max(10 % of A, d x P); a face one shell uses, over by more than 10 % but within d x P, over by more than 1.5 x B where its boundary chords' bulge B can be measured (`cad-scene`'s `bulge.ts`, `BULGE_FACTOR`; `docs/backends.md`) | always |
| c | Distance from the surface | the linear deflection d (max(d, the face's tolerance)) | off by default |
| d | Angle between normals | the angular deflection | off by default |
| e | Turned triangles: a facet against the mean of its corners' normals, as shipped | none | always |
| f | Smallest triangle angle | tess-cadio's minAngle (10 degrees by default) | always |
| g | Longest edge | off by default; 5 % of the part's size (its bounding box diagonal) in the analysis target | always |
| h | Triangle count, per face | min(ceiling, max(200,000, 64 x the face's expected count)); the ceiling 10M on desktops, 2M on phones and tablets | always |
| l | Degenerate triangles (two corners at one position) | none | always |
| i | Geometric self-intersection | not checked | |

- **The expected count (h)** is tess-cadio's: the integral over the face's (u, v) domain of sqrt(det M) of its size metric, over sqrt(3) / 4. It needs only the face's surface, domain and the deflections, so it is computed for any backend's face (cheaply: the metric's grid, no meshing). tess-cadio caps its refinement at the same number, so its faces can't pass (h).
- **Foreign backends' distance and angle (c, d)** are unchecked by default: their contract is trusted, and the record says "unchecked", never "passed". An option turns on our own sampled verification (points of each triangle inverted onto the surface with cad-geom). It is to be revisited once tess-cadio is the primary backend and a foreign backend meshes only the few faces it falls back for. **Their expected count (h)** likewise: a tess-cadio meshing's cap where there is one of the same part (the primary or the fallback in `FallbackTessellator`), else unmeasured by default; an option (`TessellationRequest.recordExpected`) turns on tess-cadio's count for its first meshing of the part, at its cost (below: 3.2 x OCCT's meshing). tess-cadio's own (c), (d), (e) and (f) come from its meshing, at no cost.
- **Unverified:** where the oracle trusts no area, (b) is unverified, not passed.

## Targets

What users see of triage is its profile, called the **target** (the word "triage" is internal only): the viewer's pulldown is labelled Target, the CLI's options say target.


Each criterion's warn and error levels, as multiples (at least 1) of its true limit. A warning keeps the face's mesh; an error withholds it (and fails an export, below).

| Criterion | Rendering | Manufacturing | Analysis |
|---|---|---|---|
| a, manifold | warn | error | error |
| b, area | warn past the true limit; error past 25 % | warn past the true limit; error past 15 % | error past the true limit |
| c, distance | warn past 1 x; error past 10 x | warn past 1 x; error past 2 x | warn past 1 x; error past 2 x |
| d, angle | warn past 1 x | warn past 1 x; error past 2 x | warn past 1 x; error past 2 x |
| e, turned | warn | warn | warn |
| f, smallest angle | warn | warn | warn past 1 x; error past 1.5 x |
| g, longest edge | warn | warn | warn past 1 x; error past 1.5 x (true limit 5 % of the part's size) |
| h, triangles | error past 1 x | error past 1 x | error past 1 x |
| i, self-intersection | allowed | allowed | allowed |

- **Rendering:** interactive viewing (the viewer's default), and also collision meshes for robotics and game engines, and offline rendering (path tracing).
- **Manufacturing:** 3D printer slicers (3MF) and mesh boolean kernels (such as manifold).
- **Analysis:** simulation (CFD, FEA), where the smallest angle and the longest edge matter.
- Every level is configurable on its own; a target only sets them all at once.
- **Built** (`cad-scene`'s `targets.ts`): the three targets as data (`TARGETS`: the levels above, the meshing limits they ask of backends, the smallest angle 10 degrees for all, analysis' longest edge 5 % of the part's size, manufacturing's and analysis' elementary faces' stretch capped at 16, (h)'s ceiling, 10M, and when the fallback is asked, `fallbackOn`), and `classify`, a face's record (below) under a target: each criterion pass, warn, error, unchecked or unverified, with its value, true limit and their ratio, and the face's overall class, the worst of pass, warn and error (unchecked and unverified ones don't make it worse). A (c) or (d) failed by an amount not measured is a warning, marked unmeasured (an error level is a multiple of a value it lacks). (h) without an expected count: passed to 200,000 triangles, an error past the ceiling, unverified between. The meshing limits go in each request (`TessellationRequest`'s `minAngle`, `maxEdge`, `elementaryStretchCap`, in tess-cache's key; tess-cadio reads them per request, `PartOptions::elementaryStretchCap` the first criteria of planes, cylinders, cones, spheres and tori; OCCT and SMLib ignore them and are only judged by them). `stepconv --describe-target <name>` prints a target's levels with each true limit's formula, its meshing limits and its fallback trigger.
- Complete (k) missing is always an error; oriented (j) is fixed where it can be (tessellateScene's orientResult turns a shell's mesh over, warning of it) and warned of; degenerate triangles (l) are dropped and counted.

## Choosing a mesh

Per face, between the primary's mesh and the fallback's (the fallback asked only where the primary's isn't acceptable: below), composed into the part's mesh by the exact join:

1. A mesh that meets every true limit: used (the primary's if both do).
2. Else a mesh with warnings but no errors under the target: used, with its warnings.
3. Else, if both have warnings only: each criterion in the order above is classed pass, warn or error, and the meshes compared criterion by criterion, first by class, then by the measured value within the class; the first criterion that differs decides; the primary's on a full tie.
4. Else (every mesh has an error): the face's mesh is withheld, with the error.

- **When the fallback is asked** (the user's decision, 2026-11-27, of the alternatives: every true limit; no error; the triggers before targets kept): a setting of each target (`Target.fallbackOn`). Rendering and manufacturing ask it only where a primary face is in error under the target; analysis wherever a primary face misses any true limit (warned of or in error), rule 1 read literally. A part the primary failed (an error, no triangles) is always sent, and is the fallback's whole where the fallback's has triangles.
- **Which faces are replaced** (the user's decision, 2026-11-27, of the alternatives: those the target wants replaced; every face where the fallback's is better by rules 1-4): once asked, only the faces the trigger names, those in error and under analysis those warned of, choose between the two meshes by rules 1-4; a face only warned of under rendering or manufacturing keeps the primary's. (Measured with every face choosing: on LMP1 #172719 one face neither backend meshes took 327 faces from SMLib and opened 21 edges.)
- **(k) and the sliver fill** (the user's decision, 2026-11-27, of the alternatives: judged on the filled mesh; judged on the backend's): a face only the sliver fill meshed passes (k) but is warned of as filled, the rest of its record measured on the filled mesh; so it sends the part to the fallback only under analysis, and isn't withheld.
- **Built** (`cad-scene`'s `FallbackTessellator`, with `TessellationRequest.target`; without one the rules before targets, `judgeResult` and `compareVerdicts`, which the corpus checks still use): both results joined, filled and oriented, their records (measured in the workers, else on this thread) classed (`classifyAll`), each side's (h) the other's tess-cadio cap where it has one; for each face the trigger names (`wantsFallback`) `chooseFace` (rules 1-4, the tie-break `compareFaces`: class, then the ratio past the limit where both have one, else the value, a count; (k)'s count and passing values not compared); the faces taken composed into the primary's and joined. A face taken that is in error once composed where its own mesh wasn't (an edge the join leaves unsewn, an error under manufacturing and analysis) is put back and the rest composed again. Never the fallback's whole mesh where the primary has one. Faces both have in error keep the primary's, which `tessellateScene` withholds.
- **Withheld** (`tessellateScene`'s `target`, a name or a `Target`; the user's decision: in every target): each part's result classed by the target, a face in error under it has its triangles left out (its range kept, with none), an error per part naming them with the criteria they fail, a warning per part counting the faces warned of by criterion. The level records the target's name and the faces withheld (`Lod.target`, `Lod.withheld`), and answers only that target (`chooseLod`, `exportView`'s `target`); the request carries the target's meshing limits, its minAngle unless `minAngle` is given.

## What errors and warnings do

- **The viewer:** an error withholds the face's mesh and shows the error modal (the problems dialog); a warning keeps the face, with no modal, the message reachable later through the About box.
- **Exports** (stepconv, and the viewer's export): an error fails the export by default; an option excludes the failing parts, exports the rest, and still reports the error. Built in stepconv: a part with a face withheld, or with no mesh, fails the target; the run exits 4 with nothing written (the summary naming the parts), `--exclude-failing` leaving them out and writing the rest (exit 0, 3 with `--strict`).
- **The CLI:** each level a named option, the target `--target <rendering|manufacturing|analysis|none>`, and `--describe-target` printing the effective settings with each true limit's formula. Its default by output format: 3MF the manufacturing target, glTF and three.js rendering, STEP none (it writes exact geometry; with `--step-meshes`, `--target` may give one). Built but for the named options per level.
- **The viewer's settings:** a dedicated modal listing each criterion with its true limit's formula and its warn and error levels.

## Open

- **The stretch cap per target (measured; capped only as a retry):** a mesher's size metric is unbounded along a direction without curvature (a cylinder's axis), so a face gets as few, long triangles as its boundary allows, and long thin triangles are where lantern bands, narrow strips' tilted triangles and slivers along the boundary come from. The metric's sizes are 3D (tess-cadio's criteria take its principal sizes in mm and pull them back through the first fundamental form), so a cap and the longest edge are 3D on every surface. Capping every face's stretch from the start costs far more than it buys (tess-cadio, natively at medium, against no cap; the user's models / a9's 654 parts): at 16, failed 19 -> 24 / 74 -> 81, past the limits 7 -> 14 / 34 -> 41, triangles 1.58 / 1.50 x, CPU 1.34 / 1.21 x; at 8, failed 23 / 82, past 17 / 44, triangles 1.90 / 1.79 x; areas mixed at 16, worse at 8 (more than 10% off 29 -> 39 on the models). The new failures are free-form faces (LMP1's B-splines) whose stretch is what lets them mesh within the point cap, refined to it with thousands of triangles turned. So the cap stays a retry (`retryStretch`, 16, after a bad mesh) for free-form faces. On elementary faces only (planes, cylinders, cones, spheres, tori) a cap of 16 from the start changes no failed, kept or past-the-limits count on either set, for 1.22 / 1.17 x the triangles and 0.98 / 0.97 x the CPU (fewer retries), areas about neutral with a few narrow faces 7-13 points worse; at 8, 1.41 / 1.34 x and areas clearly worse. Decided (the user): the manufacturing and analysis targets cap elementary faces' stretch at 16 from the start; rendering doesn't. The analysis target's longest edge (g) is the next entry.
- **The longest edge (g) in tess-cadio (built, off by default):** `Deflections::maxEdge` caps the size metric's principal sizes at maxEdge / sqrt 2 (the mesher accepts an edge up to sqrt 2 in its metric), and edges are sampled no farther apart than maxEdge; tess-cadio takes it as a fraction of the part's size (`CadioInitOptions.maxEdge`; the analysis target's 5 %). The part's size is one definition, `part_size.h`'s `partSize`, which judging any backend's mesh by (g) must take too (`CadioTessellator.partSize`): the box of the vertices, the edges at 9 points and each face's surface on a 9 x 9 grid over its loops' (u, v) box, an estimate within 0.88-1.37 x of a mesh's box on the corpus (VW #2412 and #2432 about 2.1 x), its error immaterial against a limit picked by rule. At 5 % against off (natively at medium, back to back, under load 5-22): a9's 654 parts failed faces 40 -> 41, triangles 1.165x, CPU 0.81x (long thin triangles no longer driving refusals and retries: bug29979_firetherm #168383 164 -> 34 s, LMP1 #172690 41 -> 15 s); the user's 4,062 parts failed 23 -> 25, triangles 1.40x, CPU 0.76x; triangles with a smallest angle under 5 degrees 26 % -> 22 % and 28 % -> 18 %. The newly failed face, LMP1 #172665's #1055631, is a B-spline strip whose parameterization nearly collapses along a row (|S_u| from 252 to 7.4 at u = 0.001), not a declared singularity: smallest-angle refinement runs away into the row at the cap's sizes. A pole's fan (built outside the mesher) is held to it too: its ring reaches at most maxEdge less the refinement floor and its pieces' chords are no longer, since cutting the ring into an edge may take the crossing to the edge's sample within the floor of it, past the ring (trj4_s1-tu-214 #2425, 1.004 x without it). Edges over the limit 1.72 M -> 455 and 5.44 M -> 1,142: interior edges beside the boundary that refinement won't break for encroaching it (1-2.7 x), and a few capped faces. The split rule then bisects those (`MeshOptions::bisectLong`, only with maxEdge; `docs/own-tessellator.md`, "Long edges beside segments within the size"): edge uses past the limit 439 -> 281 on a9's 654 parts (the rest on kept faces) and 602 -> 24 on the user's 2,811 (the worst 2.74 -> 1.20 x).
- **Open edges under rendering (to decide after the default switch):** with OCCT primary and SMLib fallback (the projection lane's run on f43957a, the user's models, stepconv's defaults), rendering's open edges go 50 -> 350 against no target (VW #2434 0 -> 96, LMP1 #172434 0 -> 17): with no target the judge replaces a part with open edges by the fallback's whole closed mesh, while under rendering (a) is a warning and the trigger asks on errors only, so the primary's open mesh ships. Faces taken from the fallback 11,953 -> 42, meshing wall 299 -> 229 s. The alternatives: a fallback trigger on open edges under rendering with (a) still a warning (the faces along them choosing between the meshes); (a) an error under rendering, as under manufacturing; the rule as built. To measure again with tess-cadio primary and SMLib fallback, then decide.
- Building it: the record per face, the targets and the choice by them (built); tess-cadio's keepDefects thresholds folded into the profiles; the CLI's options per level and the viewer's modal and pulldown (the viewer's agent).

## The record

Built, off by default but with a target (the choice above reads it). A request with `records` (cad-scene's `TessellationRequest.records`, with `measures` so (b) has the oracle's areas) gets `TessellationResult.records`: a row per source face id (a face a backend split summed over its pieces, a face of the model the result leaves out a row with no triangles), a typed array per value (`FaceRecords`, so a worker transfers them). `faceRecords` (`cad-scene`'s `records.ts`) measures the result as shipped, in the worker right after the request (`tess-workers`' `serve.ts`); a.2 and j exist only after the exact join and the shells' orientation, so `joinResult` and `orientResult` write them where the result has records, `fillResult` measures again the rows of the faces it filled (`recordFilled`: their measures the filled mesh's, `filled` set, their sewing, orientation and expected count kept), and `composeResults` takes each face's row from the result its mesh comes from. tess-cache keeps them with `RECORDS_VERSION`, an entry of another version not answering a request for them.

- **k, h:** the face's triangles. **h's expected count:** for tess-cadio's results, the count its point cap meshed the face to (`TessellationResult.reported`, the last meshing's), so it can't fail (h) against another number; for other backends, the cap of tess-cadio's mesh of the same part where `FallbackTessellator` has one (`shareExpected`), else with `recordExpected` tess-cadio's first meshing's (`CadioTessellator.expectedCounts`: `PartOptions::expectedOnly`, the mesher stopped at each face's cap, `expected_counts.h`), else none (unmeasured); which one in `expectedSource`. A face that fails before the count has none (not 0).
- **l:** triangles with two corners at one position (Float32, exactly).
- **a.1:** the face's mesh edges welded by position, degenerate triangles left out: edges used three times or more or twice the same way; edges used once, and of those the ones on no B-rep edge's polyline (a hole).
- **a.2:** the face's model edges along which the joined mesh is still open (`openEdges`'); -1 where the result has no polylines or wasn't joined.
- **j:** whether `orientResult` turned the face's shell (`wrongShells`: a shell judged right and one not judged both "as shipped").
- **b:** the mesh's area against the oracle's (times the face's uses by shells), by `faceFailures`' rules (`MISSING_FRACTION`, `EXCESS_FRACTION`, `BOUNDARY_SLACK`; under d x d passed; a face one shell uses over by more than 10 % but within d x P judged against `BULGE_FACTOR` x its boundary chords' bulge where the model lets it be measured, `chordBulge`), so the choice by target and the fallback's judge agree; unverified where the oracle trusts none.
- **c, d:** unchecked for foreign backends. tess-cadio reports its own: (c) passed where nothing it kept was refused for deviation, else its measured worst (`worstDeviation`), a lower bound where past the check's limit, failed unmeasured where it wasn't measured, and where its edges lie off the surface past max(d, tol) at least that distance; (d) passed where nothing was refused for its normals, else failed unmeasured (the mesher keeps no angle).
- **e:** facets against the mean of their corners' shipped normals (unchecked without normals); on tess-cadio's kept faces it equals the turned triangles it reports.
- **f:** the smallest angle in 3D and the triangles under the request's minAngle (tess-cadio's 10 degrees by default). tess-cadio's own angle counts are in its metric, not 3D, so none is taken from it.
- **g:** the longest edge, against `partSize` (`tc_part_size`), the part's size recorded with the records.

**Its cost** (2026-11-27, `workdir/2026-11-27_records/`: the user's 4,062 healed parts meshed by OCCT at medium with edges and measures, the records' steps timed in thread CPU, under load 19, so the ratios not the seconds): OCCT's meshing 405 s; the records themselves 10.3 s (2.5 % of it) and the part's size 7.7 s (1.9 %); tess-cadio's first-meshing counts for (h) 1,295 s, 3.2 x OCCT's meshing, nearly all of it tess-cadio's edge phase (sampling the edges and placing their uses, which the count's domain needs: natively, sit-stand #1663 12.3 of 13.8 s, LMP1 #172440 22.5 of 23.6 s). The records' columns are 2.3 % of the results' bytes (20.7 MB for 909 MB); a worker loading tess-cadio for them grows to at most 207 MiB. tess-cadio's own results need no counts (their caps are reported), so the cost is a foreign backend's, and only with `recordExpected`.
