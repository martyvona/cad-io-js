# Scene, importers and exporters notes

Notes for work on `cad-scene`'s model, the exporters (`export-threejs`, `export-gltf`, `export-3mf`, `stepconv`) and the mesh importers (`threejs-import`, `gltf-import`, `3mf-import`). The full record (each decision's alternatives, the phases, corpus counts, checks) is in `git show V0.2.0:CLAUDE.md` under "Decisions".

## The scene model

- Millimetres; Z up in the viewer's world; matrices column-major `Float64Array`; mesh positions `Float32Array` minus the part's origin (computed in double precision).
- One `Part` type: optional `brep` (`model`, the B-rep model's buffer: `brep-model.md`; `format: 'step' | 'iges' | 'brep'`, the source's, a closed union so the compiler shows what a new format must handle; `outline`, `vertexBox`) and `lods` (meshes, finest first: `{ mesh, from: 'source' | 'tessellated', linearDeflection?, angularDeflection?, backend? }`). Curves-only parts are a level with curves. A part with a B-rep is tessellated whatever source levels it has.
- `chooseLod` (`lod.ts`): ours at the requested settings, else the level nearest the requested linear deflection without being coarser where possible, else the finest; a level without a deflection goes after those with one. One level is used at a time (no dynamic LOD yet).
- Leaf products are part references (`leaf.ts`'s `leafParts`, run by every importer): a component whose only content is one part reference (no children, external reference or material of its own) is folded into its parents, overrides' paths following. `PartRef` carries the placement's own `name`, `sources`, `properties` and the product's (`product`); `placedName` is own, else product's, else part's. `Scene.rootParts` are placed parts at the top, before the roots in every walk.
- `InstanceOverride.ref` targets one part reference of the component at its path (what a format says per placed part round-trips exactly); `validate` checks overrides.
- `Part.closed` (true, false, absent: see `step.md`); `Part.measures` (`backends.md`, mm^2 and mm). A part has no unit (`brep-model.md`, decision 32): every importer scales meshes to millimetres as it reads them (3MF's model unit in `buildMesh`, STEP's AP242 tessellated geometry, glTF's and three.js's metres by the top nodes' turn), and our files of earlier versions that carry a part's `unit` in their extras are read with that part scaled to millimetres (`scalePart`, its placements scaled back to match). A placement that scales (a 3MF or glTF file's) stays as the file has it.
- Bounds (`bounds.ts`): `Mesh.box` in mesh coordinates over everything drawn, computed whenever a mesh is made, never read from a file. `Scene.bounds` is one sphere: from our extras when present, else estimated from each placed leaf's mesh box or vertex box; `boxesSphere` takes every placed box's 8 world corners, centre the middle of their box, radius the farthest corner (tighter than Ritter's on placed spheres). `tessellateScene` rebuilds it from meshes alone.
- `Scene.sourceUnit` (millimetres per the source's length unit): STEP's (`step.md`), 3MF's model unit, glTF and three.js metres (ours: the source's, from our extras). What STEP and 3MF exports default to (`brep-model.md`, decision 31).
- `Scene.fileAxes`: the file's own axes in the scene's, a rotation alone, set by `sceneFromTree` from its `frame` (`axesOf`): glTF's and three.js's Y up; absent for STEP, 3MF (ours too: `3mf-import` passes the identity) and our three.js files from before their up axis. The file read, not the source exported from: not in our extras (unlike `sourceUnit`). The viewer's gnomon shows it.
- Fixes for imperfect input go here (the importer, `tessellateScene`), each with a diagnostic and an opt-out, never into an exporter.
- `dropDegenerate` (default true, `dropSourceDegenerate`): every mesh importer drops triangles with two corners at one position from source levels (codecs' quantization makes many: the fuselage's Draco GLB 146,748 of 3.9 M); slivers with three distinct corners stay.

## Exports

- `exportView(scene, opts)` chooses each part's level with `opts`' deflections; `pruneView` (called by each exporter) leaves out nodes with nothing to draw (hidden parts left out count as nothing), `keepEmpty` / `--keep-empty` keeps them (refused for 3MF).
- File metadata (`Scene.properties`, `file-metadata.ts`): every importer keeps all the file says of itself and sets `author` (placeholders left out: `AUTHOR_PLACEHOLDERS`, from the corpus's STEP authors); our files with extras give the source's, the file's own as `container` (`propertiesFrom`), which `sceneJson` never writes, so a chain keeps the original. Exporters write the standard fields (`standardMetadata`) with or without extras, the rest only with them; 3MF's assembly build, having no `cadio:tree`, carries them in `cadio:properties`. Our own `cadio:` metadata isn't kept as the file's.
- Extras off by default everywhere (`extras`, `--extras`): glTF `extras`, three.js `userData`, 3MF only `cadio:` metadata. They carry closed, measures, the level's settings, placements, bounds, the source's unit, and the unit written where it isn't the format's (`fileUnit`); `measures.ts` writes and reads them.
- Transforms (`transforms.ts`): every format we read and the scene are right-handed. Beyond turns, moves and even scales: STEP's placements are rigid, but a mapped item's `CARTESIAN_TRANSFORMATION_OPERATOR_3D` can mirror (`base_axis` keeps `axis2`'s direction) with an even scale (none of our APs has a non-uniform operator; `operator()`'s `scale2`/`scale3` are defensive); glTF's nodes are TRS, uneven and negative scales allowed, no shear (a `matrix` must decompose: glTF-Transform decomposes it on reading and on `setMatrix`, so a shear is lost, said both ways); three.js stores raw matrices, but its `ObjectLoader` decomposes them unless `matrixAutoUpdate` is false (our exporter writes that for a sheared one; our importer reads as written, saying when three.js wouldn't); 3MF's are affine matrices, which the core spec (3.3, `third_party/3mf/`) allows of any kind but singular, mirrors included: a consumer keeps a mirrored shape's volume positive (its triangles' winding read turned over), as three.js draws it, so our importer and exporter keep the mesh as it is with the transform. `transformUse` counts the tree's own and the placed parts' (composition shears: an uneven scale over a turn); `EXPORT_FORMATS` in the viewer says what each format holds, and its export dialog warns of the rest. three.js draws and raycasts a mirrored mesh correctly (its winding turned with the ray: `mirrored-pick.test.ts`).
- Frames: each format keeps its convention. glTF and three.js are metres, Y up, with no root node of ours: each top node's matrix holds the turn (`yUpFrame(unit)`), so a file read and written again keeps its top matrices; with extras the scene-level data is on the glTF scene's `extras` / the three.js `Scene` object's `userData`. Older files with a root node of ours still read. 3MF is Z up, its vertices in the model's unit (divided as written, so an inch mesh written back in inches can be off in float32's last place), its transforms' translations in it and their linear parts as they are.
- **The unit written** (`unit` in every exporter's options, millimetres per unit; `stepconv --unit mm|cm|m|in|ft`; the viewer's export dialog; `LENGTH_UNITS` in `cad-scene`): STEP and 3MF default to `Scene.sourceUnit`, else millimetres (STEP snaps one within 1e-9 of a unit it names, so a file's 25.400000000000002 is written as INCH; 3MF takes only its six units: micron, millimetre, centimetre, inch, foot, metre); glTF and three.js to metres, their convention, any other written by scaling the top nodes' turn, which other readers take for metres all the same (our extras say it as `fileUnit`, so ours read it back as written).
- Hidden instances left out by default (glTF `KHR_node_visibility` by option; 3MF likewise).
- **glTF** (glTF-Transform): edges and curves each behind an option, as child nodes with their own meshes; compression Draco edgebreaker by default (position bits 14, `--gltf-draco-bits`), meshopt (`EXT_meshopt_compression` + `KHR_mesh_quantization`, 16-bit positions, our own quantization per part mesh with dequantization in the node matrix, triangles reordered within each face) or none; with Draco and extras a primitive's faces are a `_FACE` uint16 vertex attribute (edgebreaker reorders triangles; more than 65,535 faces in a primitive is a `GltfError`), meshopt and uncompressed keep index ranges. Instancing by option (`EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing`, required: every look placed twice or more, split by visibility; faces only, as three.js draws instanced lines once; root parts not instanced). Our exporter writes TRS back exactly and packs the GLB itself. metallic 0, roughness 1, 16-bit indices up to 65,535 vertices.
- **three.js JSON:** single-sided faces by default, `MeshStandardMaterial` (roughness 1, metalness 0), black edges, grey 0.8 default; colours sRGB hex; written in chunks (V8's string limit: `bug30790.step` at medium can't be read back as one string).
- **3MF:** `basematerials` by default, `colorgroup` by option, Materials extension not required; a build item per placed part by default (part number = instance path; root parts `:<index>`), `--3mf-assembly` the tree as components (a named placed part as a components object holding its mesh object, since 3MF components have no names; a product its assembly holds alone wrapped likewise). A part the source says is open is never a `model` (left out, or a `surface` with `--3mf-surfaces`); one said closed must still be a closed manifold. With extras: `cadio:tree` (the tree as JSON), `cadio:materials` (colour slots' scene materials), each object's `cadio:faces` (face ids as runs). Exact welding, an object per look, shortest float32 text, deflate level 6. Names with line breaks are written with spaces (lib3mf refuses character references).
- **STEP** (`step-export`'s `exportStep`; its rules, decisions 11 and 12 and its round trip: `brep-model.md`, phase 6): AP242, AP214 by option; the exact geometry, parts without it as AP242 tessellated geometry, B-rep parts' meshes beside them by option; nothing meshed for it otherwise (`stepconv`, the viewer).
- `stepconv`: default backend OCCT, profile medium (`--coarse`, `--medium`, `--fine`, `-d`, `--angular-deflection`); exit 0 when parts fail, `--strict` 3; options `--with-edges`, `--with-curves`, `--unlit-lines`, `--gltf-compression`, `--gltf-instancing`, `--gltf-embed`, `--keep-empty`; no default cache directory.

## Importers

- Each format its own package; shared core `cad-scene`'s `tree-import.ts` (`TreeNode`, `TreeMesh`, `TreeMaterial`, `sceneFromTree`), 3MF built directly. Every importer ends with `leafParts` and `boundScene`. Textures aren't supported: warned of and ignored.
- **With our extras** the tree comes back as written: components by our index, sharing, placements an exporter left out (hidden where another instance has them), per-placement colours, GPU instances put back by their extras' `path` and `names`, empty pruned nodes made again (a node under a component placed twice is the component of its slot, `parent/k`, shared by every instance). Transforms are matched to 1e-5 relative to the matrix's largest entry (float32 TRS; models in feet), each instance's references and children matched once each.
- **Without extras** (anyone's files): a node holding meshes and nothing else but its own `edges`, `curves`, `points` children is a placed part (its meshes merged into one part, each mesh's groups / triangle primitives its faces, numbered on, `faceMaterials` per face; an `edges` child of lines is that mesh's B-rep edges); other nodes are components; GPU instances (`EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing`, `InstancedMesh`) a placement each; glTF's scene nodes are the roots, three.js's root `Scene` object a container. Onshape writes a primitive per face, hence a part per mesh, not per primitive.
- **glTF:** glTF-Transform 4.5.1 `WebIO` with `ALL_EXTENSIONS`; draco3dgltf and meshoptimizer decoders imported only when used (the viewer gives Draco its wasm URL, `draco` option). glTF-Transform replaces `asset.generator`, so read it from the file's JSON: **Onshape writes sRGB in `baseColorFactor`** (its GLB's factors equal its STEP's), corrected for Onshape's files only. A `.gltf` whose `.bin` isn't given is an error (`files not given: ...`), not an empty scene. glTF-Transform's warnings are info, its errors warnings.
- **3MF** (`3mf-import`): our own reader (fflate's streaming `Inflate`, zip64 included; a streaming XML tokenizer over decoded text, sticky regexes; no `DOMParser` in workers); lib3mf is the test oracle. Faces: an object's triangles of one colour. Normals with a crease angle (30 degrees, `creaseAngle`, angle-weighted). `type="model"` closed, `surface` open. Production extension model parts read (units converted per part). `requiredextensions` we don't read is a warning, read anyway. Unused objects dropped. Vertices read as doubles (Onshape writes more digits than float32). Our files with extras rebuilt from `cadio:tree`; ours without extras read as anyone's (flat items). A mesh object's build item is a root part, a components object's a root occurrence. sit-stand (377 MB of XML) reads in about 6 s.
- **three.js JSON:** old files with our extras (before 2026-10-24: millimetres, no up) read in millimetres.

## Round trips

- `viewDifferences` (`compare.ts`) compares two export views placement by placement: names along the path, visibility, triangles (world corners, face id, colour), edges, curves, points. Placements paired by the world centroid of what they draw (meshopt's dequantization changes matrices), nearest first; triangles and lines matched to one within the tolerance (default 1e-4 mm) through a spatial hash, every candidate pair nearest first (greedy matching grows mismatches with the tolerance; the same lesson came back in the 3MF oracle); `skipHidden`, `faceIds: false`, `lines: false`; an unmatched triangle with two corners within tolerance is ignored.
- `roundtrip_corpus` (STEP -> scene -> each form -> import -> compare, 7 forms: three.js, GLB uncompressed, Draco, meshopt, instanced, 3MF, 3MF assembly): 517 of 517. Tolerances: 1e-4 mm lossless; twice the codec's step over the largest placed mesh's extent; instancing 8 float32 steps at the model's reach; 3MF 4 steps of the farthest a mesh's coordinates reach from its origin (`trj4_k1_geo-pe-*.stp`'s origin is 176 inches from its geometry). About 31 min, `--max-old-space-size=12000`.
- What round trips lose by design: nodes pruned for having nothing to draw; 3MF's flat items without extras; 3MF's assembly form carries no measures or sphere.

## Libraries and specs

- glTF-Transform's writer drops a node's TRS components within 1e-5 of the default (`bug29715_slow.stp`); an empty buffer is invalid glTF: remove it. The Khronos validator (2.0.0-dev.3.10) doesn't know `KHR_node_visibility`, Draco, meshopt or `EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing` (`UNSUPPORTED_EXTENSION`, `UNUSED_OBJECT`), and keeps one message per issue (many bad indices exhaust memory): checks decode and compare instead.
- **glTF compression:** edgebreaker reorders triangles and vertices (sequential keeps order, compresses about as meshopt unreordered); glTF-Transform hands Draco a primitive's whole vertex accessor, so the quantization range is the whole part mesh's; Draco quantizes float generic attributes (12 bits: face ids came back wrong), integer ones it keeps exact; its encoder skips non-triangle primitives, saying so through the document's logger; meshopt's FILTER encodes normals octahedrally and leaves positions as they are (quantize them first); meshopt gains most in vertex-cache order. `draco3dgltf` is CommonJS with browser and Node branches; its encoder `.wasm` is `draco3d`'s.
- **`EXT_mesh_gpu_instancing`:** three.js's `GLTFLoader` (0.186.1) instances only triangle primitives and draws a `LINES` or `POINTS` mesh on an instanced node once; instance transforms come before the node's (`NodeWorld * Instance * vertex`), so meshopt's dequantization goes into each instance.
- `quantize()` in `@gltf-transform/functions` pulls in sharp (28 MB native) and moves meshes to child nodes: not used.
- lib3mf 2.5.0: refuses numeric character references; ignores a triangle's `p1` without `pid`; reads transforms as float32; `IsManifoldAndOriented` passes an inside-out mesh; `ReadFromBuffer` needs a pointer (use its file system); Embind objects must be `.delete()`d.
- 3MF spec: `basematerials` colours are display-only, print intent needs `colorgroup`; an object with triangle properties needs `pid`/`pindex`; component objects mustn't have them; items' `partnumber` unique per item; metadata names outside the spec's list need a namespace; triangles' indices distinct; a `model` needs 4+ triangles, manifold, outward.
- A Node `Buffer` from `readFileSync` may be a view into a pooled `ArrayBuffer`: libraries that read `.buffer` see garbage; pass a copy.
- `String(x)` is the shortest round-trip text for doubles; Part 21 needs a `.` added and `E` without `+`; float32 needs its own search (`formatFloat32`).
